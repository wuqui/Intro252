## "What is the meaning of the word *nice*?" This question is an example of:

- ✅ Semasiology.
- ❌ Onomasiology.
- ❌ Both semasiology and onomasiology.
- ❌ Neither semasiology nor onomasiology.

**Explanation**: This is a semasiological question because it asks about the meaning of a given form (form → meaning). An onomasiological question would ask "What is the word for expressing approval?" (meaning → form).


## What is the relationship between a referent and an extension?

- ❌ They are the same thing.
- ✅ A referent is a specific entity, while extension is the class of all possible entities.
- ❌ Extension is a specific entity, while referent is the class of all possible entities.
- ❌ They are unrelated concepts.

**Explanation**: A referent is a specific entity picked out by a word in a particular context (e.g., a specific car). The extension is the class of all possible entities to which a word can refer (e.g., all cars). The referent is always a member of the extension.


## What is the main difference between denotation and connotation?

- ❌ Denotation is subjective, connotation is objective.
- ✅ Denotation is the core meaning, connotation is the emotional/associative meaning.
- ❌ Denotation applies to nouns only, connotation applies to adjectives only.
- ❌ They are the same thing.

**Explanation**: Denotation is the literal, dictionary meaning of a word (its core, objective meaning). Connotation is the emotional, cultural, or associative meaning that goes beyond the literal meaning and is more subjective and context-dependent.


## Which of the following best describes a lexical field?

- ❌ A group of words with identical meanings.
- ✅ A group of words covering different or overlapping areas within the same extralinguistic domain.
- ❌ A group of words that are all antonyms.
- ❌ A group of words from the same language family.

**Explanation**: Lexical fields are groups of words that cover different or partly overlapping areas within the same extralinguistic domain (e.g., colour adjectives, types of footwear).


## Which of the following pairs demonstrates gradable antonymy?

- ✅ *hot* – *cold*.
- ❌ *asleep* – *awake*.
- ❌ *teacher* – *pupil*.
- ❌ *dog* – *animal*.

**Explanation**: Gradable antonyms are opposite poles of a continuum with intermediate stages. *Hot*–*cold* is gradable (with stages like *warm*, *tepid*, *cool*). *Asleep*–*awake* is complementary (either-or), *teacher*–*pupil* is relational (different perspectives), and *dog*–*animal* is hyponymy.


## In the relationship between *vehicle* and *car*, *vehicle* is the hyperonym.

- ✅ True
- ❌ False

**Explanation**: A hyperonym is the more general term. *Vehicle* is more general than *car*, so *vehicle* is the hyperonym and *car* is the hyponym.


## Which of the following pairs demonstrates meronymy?

- ❌ *dog* – *animal*.
- ✅ *wheel* – *car*.
- ❌ *hot* – *cold*.
- ❌ *teacher* – *pupil*.

**Explanation**: Meronymy is a part-whole relationship. *Wheel* is a part of *car* (meronym-holonym). *Dog*–*animal* is hyponymy, *hot*–*cold* is antonymy, and *teacher*–*pupil* is relational antonymy.


## Descriptive synonyms share the same core meaning but may differ in:

- ✅ Connotations.
- ❌ Their denotational meaning.
- ❌ Their spelling.
- ❌ Their pronunciation.

**Explanation**: Descriptive synonyms share the same denotational (core, descriptive) meaning but may differ in connotations, stylistic level, regional variety, or collocations. If they differed in denotational meaning, they wouldn't be synonyms.


## What are the differences between collocations and idioms?

- ❌ Collocations are semantically opaque, while idioms have literal meanings.
- ✅ Idioms are semantically opaque, while collocations have literal meanings.
- ❌ Collocations occur more frequently than idioms.
- ❌ There are no differences between collocations and idioms.

**Explanation**: Both collocations and idioms involve words that occur together frequently. The key difference is that idioms are semantically opaque (non-transparent), meaning their meaning cannot be deduced from the meanings of their components, while collocations have literal meanings.


## Which of the following best describes the relationship between homonymy and polysemy?

- ❌ They are the same concept.
- ✅ Homonymy involves unrelated meanings, while polysemy involves related meanings.
- ❌ Polysemy involves unrelated meanings, while homonymy involves related meanings.
- ❌ Both always involve different pronunciations.

**Explanation**: Homonymy refers to words with identical form but unrelated meanings (e.g., *bass* ‘fish’ vs *bass* ‘instrument’). Polysemy refers to words with related meanings (e.g., *mouse* ‘rodent’ vs *mouse* ‘computer device’).


## What is the key feature that distinguishes homonymy from polysemy?

- ❌ Homonymy involves related meanings, while polysemy involves unrelated meanings.
- ✅ Homonymy involves unrelated meanings, while polysemy involves related meanings.
- ❌ Homonymy always involves different pronunciations.
- ❌ Homonymy only applies to nouns.

**Explanation**: The key distinction is that homonymy refers to words with identical form but unrelated meanings (e.g., *bass* 'fish' vs *bass* 'instrument'), while polysemy refers to words with related meanings (e.g., *mouse* 'rodent' vs *mouse* 'computer device'). Homonymy can involve same or different pronunciations.

**Explanation**: Homonymy refers to words with identical form but unrelated meanings. It can be total (same spelling and pronunciation, like *bass* ‘fish’ vs *bass* ‘instrument’) or partial (homophones like *see*–*sea* or homographs like *lead* /led/ vs *lead* /liːd/). The key feature is that the meanings are unrelated, unlike polysemy where meanings are related.


## In componential analysis, what is a seme?

- ❌ The entire set of semantic features of a word.
- ✅ The smallest distinctive feature of meaning.
- ❌ A type of semantic change.
- ❌ A synonym for lexeme.

**Explanation**: A seme is the smallest distinctive feature of meaning (e.g., [male], [adult], [human]). The entire set of semes of a word is called a semem.


## According to prototype theory, which statement is correct?

- ✅ Not all members of a category are equally good members.
- ❌ All category members have equal status.
- ❌ Categories have clear boundaries with no marginal members.
- ❌ Prototypical members are categorised slower than marginal members.

**Explanation**: Prototype theory states that categories have best examples (prototypes) and that members vary in how well they represent the category. Prototypical members are recognised and categorised faster in psycholinguistic experiments.


## The expression "The White House announced new policies" is an example of:

- ❌ Metaphor.
- ✅ Metonymy.
- ❌ Polysemy.
- ❌ Semantic change.

**Explanation**: This is metonymy because “The White House” stands for the people who work there (the government). Metonymy is based on contiguity (same conceptual structure), not similarity across different domains like metaphor.
