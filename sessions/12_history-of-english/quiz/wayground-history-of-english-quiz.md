# History of English — Wayground Quiz

## Which period of English is characterised by the greatest influx of French vocabulary?

- ❌ Old English
- ✅ Middle English
- ❌ Early Modern English
- ❌ Modern English

**Explanation**: The Norman Conquest in 1066 brought French-speaking rulers to England. Over the following centuries, thousands of French words entered English, particularly in domains like government, law, religion, and art. Estimates suggest 30–40% of Modern English vocabulary is of French origin, most of which entered during the Middle English period.


## The Great Vowel Shift primarily affected which aspect of English pronunciation?

- ❌ Consonants
- ❌ Short vowels
- ✅ Long vowels
- ❌ Word stress patterns

**Explanation**: The Great Vowel Shift (c. 1400–1700) was a major sound change that affected all Middle English long vowels. These vowels were either raised or diphthongised. For example, ME /iː/ in 'life' became /aɪ/. This shift is largely responsible for the discrepancy between English spelling and pronunciation today.


## Old English is classified as a synthetic language because it…

- ❌ …borrowed many words from other languages.
- ❌ …had a fixed subject-verb-object word order.
- ✅ …expressed grammatical relationships primarily through inflectional endings.
- ❌ …used many function words like prepositions and auxiliaries.

**Explanation**: Synthetic languages express grammatical relationships through inflections (word endings). Old English had extensive case, number, and gender marking on nouns, adjectives, and pronouns. In contrast, Modern English is more analytic, relying on word order and function words rather than inflections.


## Which of the following is the most accurate description of Celtic influence on English?

- ❌ Celtic contributed many everyday vocabulary words to English.
- ❌ Celtic influence shaped the grammatical structure of Old English.
- ✅ Celtic influence survives mainly in place names like London and Thames.
- ❌ Celtic borrowings entered English primarily during the Middle English period.

**Explanation**: Despite centuries of contact, Celtic influence on English vocabulary is surprisingly small. The Celts were pushed to the northern and western regions when Germanic tribes arrived. Celtic traces survive almost exclusively in place names, particularly those connected with hills (e.g., Pendle) and rivers (e.g., Thames).


## When did William Caxton introduce the printing press to England?

- ❌ 1066
- ❌ 1362
- ✅ 1476
- ❌ 1611

**Explanation**: William Caxton set up his printing press in Westminster in 1476. This technological innovation had profound effects on the English language, contributing to standardisation of spelling and punctuation, wider circulation of written works, and the emergence of a written standard.


## Which words did English borrow from Old Norse (Scandinavian)?

- ❌ Words primarily related to religion and the church.
- ❌ Words primarily related to law and government.
- ✅ Basic vocabulary including function words like 'they,' 'them,' and 'their.'
- ❌ Words primarily related to art and literature.

**Explanation**: Old Norse influence on English is remarkable for affecting basic, high-frequency vocabulary. Unlike French borrowings (often in formal domains), Scandinavian loans include everyday words and even function words: pronouns ('they,' 'them,' 'their'), verbs ('take,' 'call,' 'die'), and nouns ('law,' 'egg,' 'husband').


## During which period did English lose grammatical gender?

- ❌ Old English (c. 450–1150)
- ✅ Middle English (c. 1150–1500)
- ❌ Early Modern English (c. 1500–1700)
- ❌ Modern English (c. 1700–present)

**Explanation**: Old English had grammatical gender (masculine, feminine, neuter) like Modern German. The loss of inflectional endings in Middle English, along with the establishment of the invariable article 'the,' led to the replacement of grammatical gender with natural gender by around 1200.


## Which Germanic tribes invaded Britain in the 5th century and brought the ancestor of English?

- ❌ Franks, Goths, and Vandals
- ✅ Angles, Saxons, Jutes, and Frisians
- ❌ Vikings, Danes, and Norwegians
- ❌ Normans and Bretons

**Explanation**: The Angles, Saxons, Jutes, and some Frisians arrived in Britain from the mid-5th century onwards. They brought West Germanic dialects that would become English. The name 'English' derives from 'Angles' (OE 'Engle'), and 'England' means 'land of the Angles.'


## Why is Middle English often called "the period of levelled inflections"?

- ❌ Because inflectional endings became more complex and varied.
- ❌ Because inflections were borrowed from French and levelled across word classes.
- ✅ Because unstressed vowels in endings reduced to schwa and then disappeared.
- ❌ Because the printing press standardised inflectional spelling.

**Explanation**: In Old English, different endings like '-a,' '-u,' '-e,' '-an,' '-um' distinguished grammatical functions. Due to initial stress in Germanic words, these unstressed vowels weakened to schwa /ə/ and eventually disappeared, leaving English with far fewer inflections than its ancestor.


## Why is there often a mismatch between English spelling and pronunciation?

- ❌ English borrowed its alphabet from Greek instead of Latin.
- ✅ Spelling was largely fixed before the Great Vowel Shift changed pronunciation.
- ❌ The Norman Conquest introduced French spelling rules to English.
- ❌ Modern English has more vowel sounds than letters in the alphabet.

**Explanation**: English spelling was becoming standardised through printing in the 15th–16th centuries, just as the Great Vowel Shift was changing how vowels were pronounced. This means our spelling often reflects Middle English pronunciation rather than modern sounds.


## English belongs to which branch of the Indo-European language family?

- ❌ Romance
- ❌ Celtic
- ✅ Germanic
- ❌ Slavic

**Explanation**: English is a West Germanic language, closely related to Frisian, Dutch, and German. It belongs to the Germanic branch of the Indo-European language family. Despite heavy borrowing from Latin and French, English remains genetically Germanic in its core vocabulary and grammar.
