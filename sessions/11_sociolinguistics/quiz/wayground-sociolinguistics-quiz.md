# Sociolinguistics Quiz

## What is a "variety" in sociolinguistics?

- ❌ A language that deviates from grammatical rules
- ✅ A regionally, socially, or functionally specific form of a language
- ❌ A synonym for "dialect"
- ❌ The standard form of a language

**Explanation**: In sociolinguistics, "variety" is the most general term for any regionally, socially, functionally, or otherwise specific form of a language.


## Which of the following best describes a "dialect"?

- ❌ Any pronunciation that differs from the standard.
- ❌ A language used only in formal contexts.
- ✅ A regionally specific variety that differs from the standard in pronunciation, morphology, syntax, and vocabulary.
- ❌ A language spoken by a single individual.

**Explanation**: A dialect is spoken in a certain region and differs from the standard language in terms of pronunciation, morphology, syntax, vocabulary etc. Unlike accent, dialect involves more than just pronunciation differences.


## What is the key difference between "dialect" and "accent"?

- ❌ Dialect is regional, accent is social.
- ❌ Dialect is spoken, accent is written.
- ✅ Accent involves only pronunciation differences, dialect involves grammar and vocabulary too.
- ❌ Dialect is informal, accent is formal.

**Explanation**: An accent involves deviations from the standard only with regard to pronunciation, while a dialect differs in pronunciation, morphology, syntax, and vocabulary.


## What is a "sociolect"?

- ❌ A language spoken in a specific region
- ✅ A linguistic variety specific to a social class or group
- ❌ The language of an individual speaker
- ❌ A variety used in formal situations

**Explanation**: A sociolect is a linguistic variety specific to a social class or group, with specific characteristics in pronunciation, morphology, syntax, and vocabulary.


## Which of the following are factors that motivate sociolects?

- ❌ Regional background and communicative situation
- ❌ Dialect features and pronunciation patterns
- ✅ Socio-economic status, education, profession, ethnicity, gender, age, etc.
- ❌ Geographical distance and language contact

**Explanation**: Sociolects are motivated by multiple social factors including socio-economic status, level of education, profession, ethnicity, gender, and age. Speakers typically have one main geographical background but belong to several social groups.


## What is the relationship between social class and regional variation in British English?

- ❌ Higher social class speakers show more regional variation.
- ✅ Lower social class speakers are more likely to show regional variation.
- ❌ Social class has no effect on regional variation.
- ❌ Only middle class speakers show regional variation.

**Explanation**: The lower the social class, the more probable is a high degree of regional variation. Speakers of RP (the most prestigious accent) show no regional variation, while lower social classes tend to retain more regional dialect features.


## What percentage of the US population speaks General American (GA)?

- ❌ About 3–5%
- ❌ About 30%
- ✅ About 70%
- ❌ About 95%

**Explanation**: General American is spoken by approximately 70% of the US population. It is defined as a neutral variety, not having features from Southern or Eastern accents.


## What characterises Received Pronunciation (RP)?

- ❌ It is spoken by the majority of the British population.
- ❌ It is tied to a specific region in England.
- ✅ It carries high prestige and is spoken by only 3–5% of the population.
- ❌ It is the variety used in informal speech.

**Explanation**: Received Pronunciation (RP) is spoken by only 3–5% of the British population (typically upper class), carries high prestige, and is not tied to any specific region.


## Which statement about postvocalic /r/ is correct?

- ✅ General American pronounces /r/ in all positions, while RP only pronounces /r/ before vowels.
- ❌ RP pronounces /r/ in all positions, while GA drops /r/ after vowels.
- ❌ Both GA and RP pronounce /r/ in all positions.
- ❌ Neither GA nor RP pronounces postvocalic /r/.

**Explanation**: In General American, /r/ is pronounced in all positions (e.g., "car" /kaːr/). In RP, there is no postvocalic /r/ — it is only realised before vowels. This is why RP speakers say "car" as /kaː/ without the final /r/.


## What characterises slang?

- ❌ It is a regionally defined variety similar to dialect.
- ❌ It consists primarily of technical terms used within specific professions.
- ✅ It often involves neologisms, creative word formation, and has a short life span.
- ❌ It serves primarily to signal educational background.

**Explanation**: Slang consists of words or phrases used instead of more conventional forms as markers of group membership. It features many creative word formations and neologisms, and slang words often have a short life span.
